## ⚠️ NOTE ON SOURCE MATERIAL

The novel manuscript itself was not provided for this production package; the cover image was the source. The trailer design is therefore constructed from:

1. The cover's visual iconography (twin moons, lone lantern-bearer in a foggy ravine, skeletal wolf),
2. The title itself — "Valley of the Forgotten Wolves" / "وادي الذئاب المنسية" — which positions the horror around memory, forgetting, names, and predation,
3. The established mythos of the *Land of Zikola* series by Amr Abdel Hamid (a parallel-world fantasy centered on an otherworldly land where intellect is currency, memory is a weapon, and return is not guaranteed),
4. The explicit brief: photorealistic psychological / supernatural horror, not fantasy-adventure.

Before final rendering, the director should map each scene below against specific passages of the published novel and replace or refine any beat that contradicts the book's actual events. The skeleton is faithful to the cover identity and Zikola tone; the exact dialogue/memory beats should be cross-checked against the text.

# PHASE 1 — CINEMATIC HORROR ANALYSIS

### 1. Central storyline (as derived from cover + series DNA)
A traveler crosses into a forbidden valley on the borders of Zikola — a place so old it has been **erased from memory**, not just from maps. The wolves that inhabit it are not animals. They are the **residue of everyone the valley has forgotten**, hunting not for flesh but for **names**. To be taken by them is to be unwritten — no one will remember you existed. The protagonist must escape the valley **before his own name is erased** from his mind, from his world, and from the stone.

### 2. Primary character
- **Protagonist (tentatively "Khalid" / خالد — placeholder until verified against novel):** Male, late 20s, Egyptian, weather-beaten, traveler's coat, backpack, an old oil lantern (his father's — the lantern is a recurring symbol of memory/guidance through the Zikola books). A man who carries too many names of people he has lost.

### 3. Most terrifying moments (suitable for trailer)
- The first sight of **two moons** in the sky (proof he is no longer in his world).
- The **shadow on the wall** of a wolf cast by nothing.
- Footprints in dust that **appear in real time** around him, with no source.
- The **wall of names** — hundreds of names scratched out, one name fresh.
- The **lantern dying** as the pack closes in.
- The moment he **forgets his own mother's name** mid-whisper.
- The alpha wolf made of **blackened bone and blue ghost-fire eyes**, silent.
- The realization that the wolves are not hunting him — they are **welcoming him home**.
- The **reflection jump** (the wolf is behind him; he does not see it, but we do).
- The lantern left on the cave floor, footprints stopping mid-step.

### 4. Darkest psychological elements
- **Erasure as death** — worse than dying is being forgotten, including by yourself.
- **Doubt of your own memory** — did that person ever exist? Did I?
- **The light that betrays you** — your only protection is a beacon for what hunts you.
- **Silence as a living thing** — the absence of sound becomes a presence.
- **The self as prey** — the wolves don't eat your body; they eat your identity.

### 5. Most mysterious locations
- The Twin-Moon Ravine (entry point).
- The Valley floor with its half-buried ruins of a civilization no one remembers.
- The Cave of Names.
- The Bone Cathedral (cavern of the pack).
- The Stone that Forgets (where the final confrontation occurs — to be confirmed against novel).

### 6. Supernatural phenomena
- Two moons whose light casts **double shadows** in opposite directions.
- Shadows with no casters.
- Names on stone that **appear/vanish**.
- Skeletal wolves whose howls make you **lose memories** (no sound in the trailer — their howl is SILENCE, which is more terrifying).
- Mirrors / reflections that show what is behind you before you can see it.
- Stones that breathe.

### 7. Disturbing imaginative concepts
- A valley that is a **predator of memory**.
- Wolves made from the **bones of the forgotten**, each bone a stolen name.
- The lantern flame burns on the **oil of memory** — when it sputters, he forgets something.
- To survive, you must **let yourself be forgotten** — which is itself a kind of death.

### 8. Strongest emotional conflicts
- The will to survive vs. the terror of surviving as a stranger to yourself.
- Grief for people the world has erased.
- Trusting the light even when you know it leads them to you.
- The choice to **remember the forgotten** and thereby draw their hunters.

### 9. Most cinematic scenes
- Wide shot of twin moons over the valley.
- Protagonist lantern-lit, alone in the dark, looking back.
- The wall of names.
- The skeletal pack emerging from blue fog.
- The reflection shot (final horror).
- The abandoned lantern in silence.

### 10. Biggest unanswered questions (to leave with the audience)
- Is the protagonist already forgotten?
- Why did his name appear fresh on the wall?
- What did the alpha wolf whisper that made him freeze?
- Does anyone remember he left?
- Can he leave with his name intact?

### 11. Defining atmosphere
*Cold, blue, silent, ancient, breath-held dread.* Like walking into a graveyard whose names you can no longer read and realizing you came to visit yourself. Warm lantern-light as the only heartbeat in a world of cold moonlight. Fog that feels intelligent. The sense that something enormous is watching from behind every rock, and it is patient.

### 12. What makes it unique
- The horror is not gore or jump-scares — it is **ontological**: the terror of *ceasing to have ever existed*.
- The "wolves" are not animals; they are **the accumulated forgotten**, an archive made predatory.
- The double-moon / double-shadow visual signature is instantly iconic.
- The setting bridges Arabic literary fantasy (Zikola) with international A24-style psychological horror.
- It is a horror trailer for a book that is technically fantasy — the dissonance will unsettle viewers.

# PHASE 3 — VISUAL STYLE BIBLE

**Resolution:** 4K (3840×2160), 24fps, anamorphic 2.39:1 container framed inside 16:9 delivery.
**Camera feel:** ARRI Alexa Mini + Cooke Anamorphic/i primes (simulated). Natural lens breathing, subtle edge distortion, oval bokeh, subtle anamorphic lens flare on lantern light.
**Color:** Cold blue shadows (teal ~ 4600K key moonlight), warm amber practicals (lantern at ~2200K), near-black blacks, desaturated midtones, occasional blood-crimson accents (moons turn red in escalation). One signature color: **bone-blue** (#1a3a5c) — the luminescence of the wolves' eyes.
**Film texture:** Subtle 35mm film grain (Kodak Vision3 500T emulation), slight halation around highlights, no digital over-sharpening.
**Lighting rule:** All light is **motivated**. Moonlight (blue, overhead, soft), lantern (warm, hard, flickering, cast shadows long), wolf-eye glow (cold cyan, fill only). No scene uses a fill light that doesn't exist in-frame. Faces must retain detail at -4 stops.
**Fog:** Volumetric, physically simulated. Fog layers: ground fog (knee-height, cold, slow), mid-layer (waist-height, drifting), upper mist (around moons). Fog never looks like a card; it interacts with light beams.
**Forbidden:** Jump-cut zoom, cheap flashes, CGI over-statement, cartoon fangs, excessive gore, plastic skin, symmetry-for-symmetry, drone-style establishing shots, stock-orchestra stabs.

## SCENE 1 — THE HOOK (0:00–0:08)

**BLACK SCREEN.** 2 seconds of near-silence. A low sub-bass drone rises like breath held too long.

FADE IN: EXT. RAVINE — NIGHT. Low-angle, looking up through a jagged black rock slit in a canyon wall. TWO MOONS hang side by side — one paler, one blood-kissed peach — drifting in indigo cloud. Their light casts **two shadows** of everything.

On the cliff edge between the moons, the silhouette of a WOLF — too tall, too thin — throws back its head and howls.

We HEAR no howl. We see its jaw open in perfect silence. The frame trembles.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
*(low, slow, controlled)*
Every land has its maps.
Every map has its borders.

DISSOLVE TO BLACK.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
But there is a place beyond the borders of Zikola
that no map was ever meant to record.

## SCENE 2 — THE VALLEY (0:08–0:16)

EXT. VALLEY OF THE FORGOTTEN WOLVES — NIGHT.
Cinematic wide shot, slow push-in from a high vantage. A vast desolate valley stretches beneath the twin moons: crumbling pre-Sumerian stone ruins half-swallowed by sand, twisted dead trees like skeletal fingers, a dried riverbed of cracked earth. Thick cold ground-fog pools like a held breath. Distant impossible black architecture — spires? ribs? — rises above the fog line.

Double-shadows of rocks stretch in opposite directions. The wind does not move the fog.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
A valley the world forgot.
And the wolves that live there…
never forget a name.

A faint amber pinprick — the lantern — appears far below, moving through the ruins. It is the only warm thing in frame.

## SCENE 3 — THE TRAVELER (0:16–0:24)

INT./EXT. CAVE MOUTH — NIGHT.
Medium close-up, slight handheld. The PROTAGONIST (late 20s, Egyptian, weather-beaten, worn beige coat, backpack, oil lantern in his right hand, left hand pressed to cold stone for balance). Sweat beads on his temple. He looks back over his shoulder.

His eyes reflect twin lights: the warm lantern and the cold moon.

Slow rack focus from his face (foreground) to the empty darkness behind him (background). Nothing is there. But the focus holds on the darkness too long.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
He came looking for answers.
A light in the dark.
A lantern against the night.

CLOSE UP on his knuckles whitening around the lantern handle.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
But in the valley,
the light does not protect you.
It only tells them where you are.

He steps forward. The lantern swings. Shadows lunge across the wall — and one of those shadows is not his.

## SCENE 4 — THE FIRST WARNING (0:24–0:32)

INT. CAVE PASSAGE — NIGHT.
The lantern's beam falls on a wall of wet stone. The protagonist's shadow is cast there — and next to it, standing at his side, is a SECOND SHADOW: the outline of an enormous wolf, ribs visible, jaw unhinging. There is nothing beside him to cast it.

He freezes. Does not turn. Stares at the wall. Breathing shallow.

The wolf-shadow's jaws slowly close. Open. Close. As if tasting the air.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
First, the warnings came in shadows.
Then in silence.

CUT TO: EXTREME CLOSE UP — dust on cave floor. Paw prints appear in the dust. One. Then another. No sound. No creature visible. Prints approaching.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Then in names scratched into stone.

## SCENE 5 — REALITY BREAKS (0:32–0:40)

EXT. VALLEY — NIGHT.
The protagonist stumbles out of the cave mouth into the valley. He stops. The world around him is WRONG. The stones of the ruins appear to BREATHE — their surfaces ripple like black water. The two moons in the sky BEGIN TO MERGE, their edges softening, overlapping, then snapping back into place as if reality is correcting itself.

He clutches his head. Drops to one knee. The lantern slams against rock — and its flame HANGS SUSPENDED for a full second before falling back to wick.

Flickers of skeletal wolves appear for single frames at the edge of vision — not quite there.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Reality is thinner there.
The moons move wrong.
The dead do not stay dead.

CLOSE UP: his mouth moving — he is whispering a name, trying to remember it. He cannot. A tear cuts through dust on his cheek.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
And the wolves… they remember.

## SCENE 6 — THE DISCOVERY (0:40–0:48)

INT. CHAMBER OF NAMES — NIGHT.
Over-the-shoulder shot. The protagonist's lantern-light spills across an enormous subterranean wall covered floor-to-ceiling in names carved into stone — thousands of them. Every visible name has been SCRATCHED OUT with furious repeated strokes. Except ONE.

One name, glowing pale bone-blue, carved FRESH. Arabic script. Dust falling from it as if just chiseled.

He reaches. His trembling hand almost touches. Stops. He reads it. His face breaks.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Then he found it.
A wall of names.
Hundreds of names.

CLOSE UP on his eyes — the blue glow of his own name reflected in them.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
And at the center…
carved fresh as if whispered by the stone itself —

The VO lands in near-silence, barely breathed:

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
…his own.

## SCENE 7 — THE NIGHTMARE (0:48–0:56)

INT. BONE CATHEDRAL — NIGHT.
Wide shot. The lantern beam swings upward and reveals — deep in a vast cavern, twin moonlight pouring through a fissure in the ceiling — a PACK. Dozens of WOLVES made of blackened bone. Ribcages exposed. Long bone-claws on stone. Eyes burning with faint blue fire. They stand motionless, all facing the same direction. All facing him.

At their center, on a throne of piled bones, the ALPHA — massive, antler-like bone spurs along its spine, jaws that unhinge too far — opens its mouth. NO SOUND comes out. But the LANTERN FLAME DIPS ALMOST TO NOTHING as if the air has been pulled from the room.

The protagonist stumbles backward.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
In the dark beneath the valley, they were waiting.
Not wolves as you know them.
Bones older than memory.
Hunger older than death.

Insert: EXTREME CLOSE UP (0:56–0:58) — the lantern slips from his hand, hits stone. The lens cracks. The flame gutters. Paw prints appear in the dust in a circle around it.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
And they were awake.

## SCENE 8 — THE ESCALATION (0:58–1:04)

FAST MONTAGE (each shot 0.5–1.5 seconds, building pace, handheld energy):

- The protagonist running through a narrow canyon passage, lantern swinging, warm light raking across rock walls.
- A skeletal wolf lunges through fog — jaws, ribs, blue eyes. **NO JUMP SCARE CUE**; movement is silent.
- Ancient stone doors covered in Arabic inscriptions SLAMMING shut of their own accord, inches behind him.
- The two moons turning CRIMSON simultaneously.
- The wall of names — new names scratching themselves into stone with no tool visible.
- A quick glimpse: his own mother's face — but he cannot place her name.
- A skeletal paw landing on the stone directly behind his foot as he runs.

Sound design ramps: heartbeats, wind, rising sub-bass, distant metallic scraping, a single dissonant string swell rising but never resolving.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
The valley does not let go.
The valley does not forgive.
And once it knows your name —

CUT TO BLACK, INSTANTLY, mid-sentence.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
(whispered, from black)
— it will never, ever forget it.

## SCENE 9 — THE SILENCE (1:04–1:12)

**BLACK.** Hold for TWO FULL SECONDS. Total silence. No music. No breath. No drone. The audience should check their headphones.

FADE IN: A long empty dark corridor. The protagonist's lantern sits abandoned on the stone floor, flame flickering low, casting a trembling warm circle. The corridor stretches into pitch black at both ends.

Footprints in the dust lead away from the lantern. They stop MID-STEP, three feet from the lantern, as if whoever made them was pulled upward or simply ceased.

No person visible. No wolf visible.

Hold the shot. Static camera. The flame flickers. A single dust mote floats across the beam.

A LOW SOUND begins — so low it is felt more than heard — a single sustained cello note.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
(a near-whisper, after a long silence)
…You thought you were alone.

## SCENE 10 — THE FINAL HORROR (1:12–1:20)

EXTREME CLOSE UP — the protagonist's face. He is pressed back against a wall, breathing hard, eyes clenched shut. He opens them. Stares straight ahead. He sees nothing but dark.

Slow, almost imperceptible RACK FOCUS from his eyes to the lantern glass just in front of him, reflecting what is behind him.

In the reflection: THE ALPHA WOLF is standing directly behind him, inches from his neck. Its jaws are slowly, slowly opening. Bone-teeth descending toward the back of his neck. Its blue eyes lock into the reflection — lock directly into US, the audience.

The protagonist is unaware. He lifts a trembling hand to wipe sweat from his brow. The wolf's jaw opens wider in perfect unison.

HOLD for four beats. No music. Just heartbeat. No jump. No cut. Just hold.

The lantern flame is snuffed out as if by a breath.

BLACK.

**[NO DIALOGUE.]**

Hold black for two seconds. In the darkness: a single, quiet, almost-human WHISPER of a name we cannot quite make out. Then silence.

# PHASE 6 — SOUND DESIGN

### Core philosophy
Silence is a weapon. The audience should be afraid of what they *might* hear next. Every sound is motivated. The wolves' howl is NEVER heard — when they howl, the sound is **silence** (air sucked from the room). The wolves communicate telepathically through memory, not sound.

### Palette
- **Drone:** Single sustained sub-bass tone (35Hz, sine) that rises so slowly the audience doesn't notice it until it's unbearable.
- **Room tone:** Unnatural cave resonance — low metallic ring, faint distant wind that never gets louder.
- **Breath:** The protagonist's breathing, close-mic'd, slightly unsteady, present in every scene after Scene 3.
- **Footsteps:** His boots on stone — with occasional FOOTSTEPS THAT ARE NOT HIS, slightly off-tempo, always behind him, never resolving.
- **Stone:** Subtle grinding/breathing stone sounds during Scene 5 (reality break) — faint, organic, geological.
- **Names in Scene 6:** A nearly subsonic CHISEL sound as each name appears — but the chisel is moving faster than any hand.
- **Pack in Scene 7:** Bones CLICKING softly as they move — not threateningly loud; the subtlety is worse. No growls. No snarls. Just clicking.
- **Silence in Scene 9:** Complete digital zero for 2 seconds. Then a single room-tone tone begins 6dB lower than audible threshold, rising over 8 seconds.
- **Final reflection (Scene 10):** Heartbeat, slowing. A single bone-click so soft it might be a joint in his neck. Then the flame goes out with a *thump* (not a blow — a thumbprint of air). Then the whispered name — unintelligible, genderless, right in the viewer's ear.
- **Book reveal:** Single low piano chord (A minor, root + fifth + maj7, no attack — swells in), held. Faint dust-mote sparkle. The VO comes in clean over it.

### What we AVOID
- "Inception" BRAAAMs.
- Overused trailer risers.
- String stabs on jump scares (we don't do jump scares — the dread is the scare).
- Constant music. The score breathes. The score leaves.
- Wolf howls. (Silence is their howl.)

### Music reference
- Hildur Guðnadóttir (*Joker*, *Chernobyl*) — cello-based, harmonic pressure.
- Mica Levi (*Under the Skin*) — predatory string drones, alien semiotics.
- Ben Salisbury & Geoff Barrow (*Annihilation*) — uncanny acoustic + synth blend.
- Traditional Arabic ney and oud, processed and reversed so they are felt but not recognizable.

### SHOT 02 — THE VALLEY (8s)
**Model:** Kling 1.6 or Veo 3.

> Ultra-photorealistic cinematic wide establishing shot. HIGH VANTAGE from a 40-meter cliff edge looking down into a vast desolate canyon valley at night under two moons. Ancient crumbling stone ruins of an unknown pre-Sumerian civilization half-buried in dust and sand. Twisted dead trees like skeletal fingers rise from a cracked dry riverbed. Thick knee-height volumetric ground-fog pools across the valley floor, slowly drifting. Distant impossible black architecture — spires that look like giant ribs — rise above the fog line. Double-shadows of every rock stretch in two opposite directions because of twin moonlight. A single tiny warm amber PINPRICK OF LIGHT — a man's oil lantern — moves very slowly through the ruins far below, the only warm thing in frame. # HOW TO MAKE IT MORE FRIGHTENING, MYSTERIOUS, AND MEMORABLE

1. **Trust silence.** The scariest thing in any horror film is when the audience realizes the sound has stopped and they don't know when it happened. The 2-second digital zero at 1:04 is the single most important creative decision in this trailer. Fight for it.
2. **No jump scares.** The audience will EXPECT a jump at 1:18. Don't give it to them. The wolf doesn't lunge. The flame simply goes out. Unfulfilled tension is worse than release.
3. **Let the wolf blink once in the final reflection.** Just one blink. At the exact moment the audience thinks they're staring at a statue. It makes them doubt their eyes.
4. **The whispered name at 1:19 must be YOUR name if possible.** For interactive screenings / AR versions, binaurally whisper the viewer's name. For standard release, whisper a name that is slightly unintelligible — every viewer hears their own.
5. **Never show a wolf attack.** Attacks release tension. Anticipation holds it. The wolf is always APPROACHING, never ARRIVING.
6. **The sound mix should make people check over their shoulders.** Place the off-tempo footsteps panned slightly behind the viewer's head (binaural / Atmos).
7. **Thematic marketing hook:** "What is your name?" — billboards with only that line, no title, no author, until a week before reveal when the wolf-eye image is added.
8. **Easter egg for Arabic readers:** the names on the wall (Scene 6) should include the names of the protagonist's companions from *Land of Zikola* Books 1 and 2 — all scratched out. Fans will catch it; new viewers will feel the weight of something they don't yet understand.
9. **A 4th wall break (subtle):** at 1:16 (reflection shot), the alpha wolf's eyes lock directly to camera, directly to the viewer. The protagonist does not see the wolf. But the wolf sees US. This is the psychological bridge that makes the viewer personally hunted.
10. **The final frame (1:28):** a single blue eye-blink in black. Hold for 1 full second after. Audiences will remember this blink for days.
